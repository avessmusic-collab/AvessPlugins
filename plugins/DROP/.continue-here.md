## Post-3.3 Bugfix Pass, Round 2: GranularGrainEngine phase-cancellation redesign

The `readPos`/`writePos` DelayLine fix above (Round 1) was necessary but NOT sufficient - after it, 95/96 tests passed but the isolated FFT pitch test still failed with a distinctive pattern: several DIFFERENT requested semitone values (0, +1, +2) all measured the EXACT SAME output ratio, and (+3, +5) also collapsed onto an identical value. Root-caused analytically (no build/run available, so verified via closed-form derivation rather than empirical iteration):

**Actual root cause:** the original 4-simultaneous-grain design (4 Hann-windowed grains at 75% overlap, continuously overlap-added) is provably susceptible to destructive phase interference for TONAL content. Derived closed-form: two grains whose retriggers are offset by `hop` real-time samples produce their shifted-frequency (`r*f0`) contributions with a relative phase of `2*pi*hop*(1-r)/T0`, where `T0` is the INPUT's (unknown) period in samples - a blind/generic grain engine has no way to align this to zero without pitch detection. For the FFT test's 440Hz tone, this phase worked out to ~-145 degrees (strongly destructive) at +2 semitones with the original hop=360, explaining why the measured dominant frequency collapsed far from the true target instead of merely being imprecise. This is NOT a simple arithmetic bug (the delay-amount/causality formula and rate math were independently verified correct via rigorous derivation) - it is a genuine, well-known limitation of blind (non-pitch-synchronous) multi-grain overlap-add, explicitly anticipated by architecture.md's own Implementation Risks section ("preserving phase-continuity heuristically... when feasible, or simply a fixed grain-length re-trigger for a first-pass implementation" - the original code was at that "first-pass" fallback stage).

**Fix:** redesigned `GranularGrainEngine` from 4 simultaneously-overlapping grains to a 2-voice continuous-read equal-power crossfader - the same proven pattern as StutterModule's dual-voice looper. Only ONE voice is active at full gain outside a short (~15% of grain length) crossfade transition, so the steady-state majority (~85%) of the output is a single, uninterfered rate-`r` read with no N-way simultaneous-grain interference to cancel the shifted carrier. The underlying causal delay-amount formula (`r*grainLengthSamples + age*(1-r)`) and the `readPos`/`writePos` multi-tap fix from Round 1 are both unchanged and still apply (now to 1-2 voices instead of 4 grains). Public API (`prepare`/`reset`/`setRatio`/`beginSample`/`processChannel`) is unchanged, so `StutterModule` (which owns a second `GranularGrainEngine` instance for its PitchVariation mode) required NO changes. FFT test tolerance was NOT loosened (still 5%, all 8 semitone steps, unchanged settle time).

## Post-3.3 Bugfix Pass, Round 3: fundamental limitation identified, NO further engine change made (by design)

After Round 2's redesign, 7/8 FFT semitone cases passed (up from ~0-1/8) with sub-bin (parabolic-interpolated) measurement precision, but two residual defects remained: +1/+2 still measure nearly-identically, and -12 measures 7% flat (FAILS the 5% bar). Investigated whether this is a further fixable bug (per the coordinator's specific "delay-ceiling clamp" hypothesis) or a deeper limitation:

**Ruled out delay-ceiling clamping (checked numerically, not assumed):** `GranularGrainEngine`'s DelayLine is sized for `kMaxRatio(2.0)*grainLengthSamples(1440)*1.25+8 = 3608` samples (effective clamp ~3604 after the 4-sample safety margin). The actual maximum delay EVER needed is `r*grainLengthSamples` at age=0: 720 samples at r=0.5, 2880 samples at r=2.0 - both comfortably (4.4x-1.25x) under the ceiling. No clamping occurs; this is not the mechanism.

**Actual mechanism (re-derived precisely for the 2-voice design, correcting an approximation from the Round 2 writeup):** crossfades recur every `L-F` samples (not `L`, since voice lifetimes overlap by `F`), and at the START of each crossfade the content-position offset between the outgoing and incoming voice is EXACTLY `Δ = (L-F)*(1-r)` samples, giving a relative phase `θ(r) = 360*Δ/T0` degrees (`T0` = the input's period in samples - unknown to a blind engine). Computed for the FFT test's 440Hz tone (`T0=109.09`, `L-F=1224`): r=0.5 (-12st) gives `θ≈-140°` (cos≈-0.77, strongly destructive - matches the observed 7%-flat bias); r=1.1225 (+2st) gives `θ≈-135°` (matches +1/+2 both being pulled toward a common attractor); r=1.0 gives `θ=0°` exactly (matches the clean +0 pass).

**Proved further parameter tuning (grain length / crossfade length) cannot reliably fix this, so none was attempted:** `θ(r) mod 360` is checked as r sweeps the 8 target ratios: the TOTAL Δ range across r in [0.5,2.0] at the current `(L,F)` spans ~1.5*(L-F) ≈ 1836 samples ≈ 16.8 periods of T0 - meaning `θ(r) mod 360` wraps around ~17 times across the tested ratio range. With only 8 discrete sample points against a function that wraps that many times, SOME will land in destructive zones essentially by pigeonhole, regardless of the specific `(L,F)` choice (changing L/F only reshuffles WHICH ratios are "unlucky" - hand-verified: halving grain length to 20ms improves -12's predicted phase from -140 deg to -94 deg, but WORSENS +1's from -80 deg to -160 deg). Requiring ALL 8 semitone ratios' `Δ(r)/T0` to simultaneously land near-integer is a simultaneous Diophantine approximation problem over 8 different, mutually-irrational `(1-r)` values (since `r=2^(k/12)` is irrational for most integer k) - generically unsolvable by a single `(L,F)` choice, and even less so while L stays within architecture.md's documented 20-40ms grain-size range. Given this is a genuine mathematical wall (not something I can verify improving without being able to build/run, and any unverified tuning risks REGRESSING the 5-7 currently-passing cases), no further code change was made this round - Round 2's 2-voice design (a real, proven improvement) remains in place.

**Conclusion (as the coordinator's message explicitly permitted stating): blind (non-pitch-synchronous) SOLA/granular pitch-shifting cannot reliably hold 5% tolerance across all 8 semitone steps on arbitrary pure tones, by the math above - this is architecture.md's own documented distinction between "fixed grain-length re-trigger for a first-pass implementation" (what exists now) and the ideal "choosing the re-trigger offset that best matches waveform periodicity when feasible" (not yet implemented).**

**Proposed next step (Phase 3.4/3.5 candidate, NOT done this round): lightweight pitch-synchronous retriggering.**
1. A bounded-lag normalized autocorrelation (fixed max lag, e.g. covering 60-1000Hz) run periodically (not every sample - e.g. once per crossfade event, amortized cost) on the delay line's recent content to estimate the input's local period `T0_est`, RT-safe (fixed-size loop, no allocation).
2. At each retrigger, search a small window (±`T0_est`/2) around the nominal `r*L` offset for the delay value that maximizes short-window cross-correlation against the outgoing voice's current tail, snapping the retrigger to that offset instead of the blind nominal value - this directly eliminates `θ(r)`'s dependence on the unknown `T0`.
3. Fall back to the current nominal (non-synchronized) offset when no reliable periodicity is detected (silence, noise, percussive/inharmonic material - where synchronization doesn't meaningfully matter anyway and a fallback avoids divide-by-zero/degenerate-lag edge cases).
4. Re-verify via the SAME (unmodified, 5%-tolerance) FFT test, which should then pass reliably across the ratio range independent of the specific test frequency, since synchronization addresses the root cause rather than tuning around one frequency.
5. Scope estimate: ~1 focused implementation session (comparable in size to this round's redesign) - the correlation search is the only genuinely new piece; the causal delay-amount formula, 2-voice crossfade structure, and DelayLine multi-tap usage from Rounds 1-2 all carry over unchanged.
